# Extracting and specifying a spine system

Never use “high detail”, “realistic spine” or “all parts attached” as a substitute for the following information.

1. Identify the binding family and evidence: observed image/text, documented historical comparison, estimated, proposed or unknown. Distinguish original binding from later repair. Describe the text-block spine and external covering spine separately. A visible gap is not automatically a hollow-back construction.
2. Set units, axes, gravity, pivots, reference pose, material coordinates, board/text-block dimensions and intended closest inspection distance. Name the supported motion range. Distinguish a static book prop from a moving hero object.
3. Describe every component from front, rear, side, head, tail and underside. Give thickness, profile, paring, layers, overlap and edge termination. Include spine folds, sewing, supports, adhesive/lining, joints, shoulders, board lacing, endpapers, endbands, headcaps and tailcaps.
4. Specify each interface by named parts and local attachment regions. Say whether bonded, sliding, flexible, touching, intentionally separated or unresolved. Describe how the support continues into the board and how folded leaves continue across each gathering. Mark permitted penetration only in specific attachment regions. Do not exempt entire categories.
5. Describe movement of both sides of every interface. Identify neutral/bending surfaces, regions allowed to compress or slide, minimum bend radius, clearance and thickness changes. State how opening affects the joint, endpaper, caps, gutter, shoulders and stack distribution. Keep material properties unknown unless measured or sourced.
6. Describe external support, contact pads, imposed opening controls, gravity and balance. An externally held pose is not evidence of equilibrium. Do not require a full simulator until the intended material behaviour is specified.
7. Describe repair location, causal damage, attachment and wear chronology. Handling polish, joint fatigue and cap abrasion must occur where use would produce them. Replacement material must follow the surface it repairs through motion.
8. Declare which details are geometry and which are surface maps. Silhouette, physical separation, contact, page thickness, caps and significant recesses require geometry. Record grain scale, direction and separate colour/roughness/normal channels.
9. Specify inspection views that actually reveal the named component. Provide labelled complete-component isolation, exploded views or sections for concealed construction; never silently expose a cut surface as finished furniture.
10. State source/export/inline/LOD contracts. Preserve dimensions, IDs, units and supported state. Distinguish a dimensioned SVG illustration from a closed 3D solid, and a specification export from a mesh export.
11. Require independent geometry, attachment, movement, appearance and functionality evidence. Use deliberate failing fixtures. Report pass, rework, not applicable or not established; untested conditions never pass. Give severity and confidence separately.
12. Specify exact browser, input, DPR, viewport and drawing-buffer conditions for runtime claims. Synthetic touch does not validate a physical device. Separate ordinary, close and supersampled performance. Preserve user state on rejected imports.

For each component deliver: **description → evidence → modelling method → interfaces and movement → verification → uncertainty**. Do not invent concealed historical construction without marking it proposed.
